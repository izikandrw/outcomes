# Definition

The quantified form of an outcome or an impact: what gets counted, where it starts, and where it has to get to.

An outcome names a behavior change in words. A target puts numbers on it. "Admins invite a teammate in their first session" is an outcome; "admins inviting in their first session, from 412 to 600, by December 31" is a target.

A target fixes three things: the unit being counted, the baseline it starts from, and the value it should reach. Without a baseline there is no progress to report, only a running total. Without a target value there is nothing to reach, only a chart. A date is the fourth thing, and a target without one is a wish — the schema allows it, the practice shouldn't.

The baseline is a reading, not a guess. It is what the number was when the team decided to move it, which is why a target is usually born next to a measurement.

An outcome can carry more than one target. A behavior change that moves exactly one number is rare: "admins invite a teammate in their first session" might be counted both as a share of new admins and as a median time to first invite, and both have to move before the outcome is real.

A target is part of what it quantifies, not a separate thing beside it. It is created, edited and shown inside its outcome or impact, and it has no life of its own: "Grow Revenue" measured as 1000 → 1200 ARR is one impact with one target, not an impact and a second "Revenue Growth" that happen to be linked. For that reason a target's name is optional. When it has none, its unit labels it; a name is only worth giving when the parent has several targets and they need telling apart.

A target can be wrong, and revising one is normal. It should be a deliberate edit rather than a quiet one, because the baseline is the record of what the team believed when it started.

A target is not a project. It says how far the behavior has to move, never what will be built to move it. If a statement describes work, it belongs to a project.

# Relationships

Targets are connected to exactly one parent: either one outcome or one impact, never both. A number with no behavior or business result behind it is a metric, not a target.

A target is owned by that parent and is never shared. Two outcomes, or an outcome and an impact, that watch the same metric each carry their own target, with their own baseline and goal, because the baseline records what that owner's team believed when it started. A parent that still has targets cannot be deleted until they are removed, so readings are never silently lost.

Targets do not nest and do not connect to each other. An outcome or impact that needs breaking down is broken into outcomes or impacts; its targets stay flat. A parent impact's target is its own number, not the sum of its children's.

Targets can be connected to one or more measurements. Those measurements are the readings taken against the target, and their order over time is its progress.

Targets are not connected to teams or projects directly. A target on an outcome inherits both through that outcome; a target on an impact reaches them through the outcomes linked to that impact.
