# Definition

The quantified form of an outcome, an impact or a project: what gets counted, where it starts, and where it has to get to.

An outcome names a behavior change in words. A target puts numbers on it. "Admins invite a teammate in their first session" is an outcome; "admins inviting in their first session, from 412 to 600, by December 31" is a target.

A target fixes three things: the unit being counted, the baseline it starts from, and the value it should reach. Without a baseline there is no progress to report, only a running total. Without a target value there is nothing to reach, only a chart. A date is the fourth thing, and a target without one is a wish — the schema allows it, the practice shouldn't.

The baseline is a reading, not a guess. It is what the number was when the team decided to move it, which is why a target is usually born next to a measurement.

An outcome can carry more than one target. A behavior change that moves exactly one number is rare: "admins invite a teammate in their first session" might be counted both as a share of new admins and as a median time to first invite, and both have to move before the outcome is real.

A target is part of what it quantifies, not a separate thing beside it. It is created, edited and shown inside its outcome, impact or project, and it has no life of its own: "Grow Revenue" measured as 1000 → 1200 ARR is one impact with one target, not an impact and a second "Revenue Growth" that happen to be linked. For that reason a target's name is optional. When it has none, its unit labels it; a name is only worth giving when the parent has several targets and they need telling apart.

A target can be wrong, and revising one is normal. It should be a deliberate edit rather than a quiet one, because the baseline is the record of what the team believed when it started.

A target is not a project. It says how far a number has to move, never what will be built to move it. If a statement describes work, it belongs to a project. A project's own target says what that work has to reach, e.g. "the self-serve checkout brings in $200K of ARR".

# Relationships

Targets are connected to exactly one parent: one outcome, one impact or one project. A number with no behavior, business result or body of work behind it is a metric, not a target.

A target is owned by that parent and is never shared. Two outcomes, or an outcome and a project, that watch the same metric each carry their own target, with their own baseline and goal, because the baseline records what that owner's team believed when it started. A parent that still has targets cannot be deleted until they are removed, so readings are never silently lost.

Targets do not nest and do not connect to each other. An outcome or impact that needs breaking down is broken into outcomes or impacts; its targets stay flat. A parent's target is its own number, not the sum of its children's. The two are compared rather than merged: targets roll up. An impact shows what share of its target its child impacts, outcomes and projects account for; an outcome, its child outcomes and projects; a project, its child projects. That adds up the target values of children in the same unit, so a plan that doesn't reach the parent's number shows it. The map draws that plan beside the target's latest reading, planned and actual on one scale from zero to the goal.

A child with no target in that unit passes on what its own children add up to; one with a target counts that number alone, so nothing beneath it is counted twice. A project that drives several parents counts in full toward each, because each parent's plan is compared separately rather than split between them. Only the first target in a matching unit counts, and a unit no parent is watching rolls up to nothing.

Targets can be connected to one or more measurements. Those measurements are the readings taken against the target, and their order over time is its progress.

Targets are not connected to teams directly. A target on an outcome or project inherits its team through that parent; a target on an impact reaches teams through the outcomes and projects linked to that impact.
