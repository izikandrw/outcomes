# Definition

A single observed value of the behavior a target counts, at a point in time.

A measurement is a reading, not a target. It records what the number was, not what it should be. "412 admins invited a teammate in their first session" taken on a given date is a measurement; "get to 600" is not.

A measurement is a value and a time. The value is what was counted, and the time is when the reading applies — which is not necessarily when it was entered. What the value counts is not stored on the reading: the unit comes from the target, so every measurement in a series is guaranteed to count the same thing.

Measurements accumulate rather than overwrite. A target's progress is the series of its measurements read in order; correcting a bad reading means replacing that reading, not editing the series.

A measurement is evidence about a target that already exists. That distinguishes it from an insight, which is evidence about whether an outcome should exist at all.

# Relationships

Measurements are connected to exactly one target. A reading with no target has nothing to be measured against.

Measurements do not nest and do not connect to each other. A series is just the readings on one target, ordered by time.

Measurements are not connected to outcomes, impacts, teams or projects directly. They inherit them through their target, which belongs to either an outcome or an impact.
