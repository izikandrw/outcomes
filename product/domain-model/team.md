# Definition

A group of individuals formed for a particular purpose.

A team is the unit of accountability. Outcomes and projects both hang off a team, so a team answers two questions at once: what behavior are we trying to change, and what are we building to change it.

Teams can be infinitely nested. A parent team is usually a group, department or division; its children are the teams doing the work. Nesting exists so a parent's outcomes can be read as the roll-up of its children's.

A team can have a logo, so it is recognisable at a glance on the team map.

Teams outlive the work. Projects finish and outcomes are achieved; the team persists and picks up the next one.

# Relationships

Teams can contain one or more teams.

Teams belong to at most one parent team. A team with no parent is a top-level team.

Teams can be connected to one or more outcomes they are accountable for.

Teams can be connected to one or more projects they are running.

Teams can be connected to one or more insights they have recorded.
